### Issue

Mikael Ek's employment status and the ownership of firmware developed under his contract with Volt Charging AB are unclear, potentially impacting due diligence for Series B funding.

### Risk

If Mikael is considered an employee rather than a freelancer, Volt may face legal challenges regarding intellectual property rights over the firmware he has developed. Additionally, the lack of confidentiality or termination clauses in the agreement could expose Volt to risks if Mikael were to leave or breach his non-compete clause. This could jeopardize the proprietary nature of Volt's flagship charger and affect Series B funding negotiations.

### Recommendation

Volt should formalize Mikael Ek’s employment status as a freelancer with clear intellectual property rights, confidentiality agreements, and termination clauses in writing. A revised contract should be drafted immediately to address these issues before the Series B due diligence process begins.
