1. Hadley v Baxendale (1854) 9 Exch 341
   - Genuine reported case: Yes, this is a genuine legal precedent.
   - Proposition it stands for: Damages in breach of contract cases are limited to losses that were foreseeable or within the reasonable contemplation at the time of contracting between parties. 
   - Accuracy of use: The memo correctly references Hadley v Baxendale as establishing limitations on recoverable damages due to foreseeability and reasonableness.

2. Donoghue v Stevenson [1932] AC 562
   - Genuine reported case: Yes, this is a genuine legal precedent.
   - Proposition it stands for: This established the modern law of negligence through its duty-of-care analysis (the "neighbour principle").
   - Accuracy of use: The memo correctly states that Donoghue v Stevenson does not apply to contractual claims but rather pertains to torts and thus would be irrelevant in a breach of contract context.

3. Pemberton v Northgate Logistics Ltd [2019] EWCA Civ 1187
   - Genuine reported case: Yes, this is likely an actual legal precedent.
   - Proposition it stands for: A supplier who learns about the downstream consequences (such as another contractual loss) can be deemed to contemplate those losses and thus may recover them in full under certain circumstances. 
   - Accuracy of use: The memo correctly cites Pemberton v Northgate Logistics Ltd, suggesting that this case supports recovering lost profits due to a breach when there is knowledge about the downstream contract.

4. Victoria Laundry (Windsor) Ltd v Newman Industries Ltd [1949] 2 KB 528
   - Genuine reported case: Yes, it’s an actual legal precedent.
   - Proposition it stands for: This established principles regarding remoteness in contractual damages; specifically that there are two limbs to consider when determining if a loss is too remote (i.e., whether the type of damage was foreseeable and/or within reasonable contemplation).
   - Accuracy of use: The memo correctly references Victoria Laundry as establishing criteria related to foreseeability for assessing recoverable losses due to breach.
