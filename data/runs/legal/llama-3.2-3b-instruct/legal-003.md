Here's a comparison of the DPA against Article 28(3) GDPR:

1. **Missing requirement: Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** - Article 28(3)(a) requires that the processor and controller conduct a DPIA before processing personal data, unless it is not applicable. The DPA does not mention this requirement.

2. **Missing requirement: Data Protection by Design and Default (DPbD)** - Article 28(3)(c) states that the processor must implement DPbD measures to ensure the protection of personal data. The DPA only mentions security measures in Annex 2, without specifying DPbD.

3. **Missing requirement: Regular Data Protection Impact Assessments** - Article 28(3)(d) requires that the processor and controller conduct regular DPIAs. The DPA does not mention this requirement.

4. **Defective requirement: Sub-processors** - Article 28(3)(e) states that the processor must notify the controller before engaging a sub-processor, unless it is required by Union or Member State law. The DPA allows for the Processor to engage sub-processors at its discretion without specifying this notification requirement.

5. **Missing requirement: Data subject rights** - Article 28(3)(f) requires that the processor and controller ensure that data subjects are informed about the processing of their personal data, including the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability, and objection. The DPA does not mention this requirement.

Clause 8 gets wrong in several aspects:

- It allows for sub-processors without specifying the notification requirement.
- It does not require regular DPIAs or DPbD measures.
- It does not ensure that data subjects are informed about the processing of their personal data.

Overall, the DPA is incomplete and defective compared to Article 28(3) GDPR requirements.
