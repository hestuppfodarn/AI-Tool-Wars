 Comparing the Data Processing Agreement (DPA) provided with Article 28(3) GDPR, the following are the missing or defective requirements:

1. a) Written form (Article 28(3)(a)): The DPA should explicitly state that the processing is carried out under a written contract or other legal act binding the processor to the data controller. The current agreement does not mention this requirement.
2. b) Obligation of confidentiality (Article 28(3)(b)): While the DPA mentions confidentiality, it should explicitly state that the processor ensures that any person acting under its authority who has access to or processes personal data shall do so only on instructions from the controller.
3. c) Respect for the rights of data subjects (Article 28(3)(c)): The DPA does not specify how the processor will assist the controller in fulfilling its obligations regarding data subjects' rights, such as access, rectification, erasure, and objection.
4. d) Cooperation with supervisory authorities and data protection impact assessments (Article 28(3)(d)): The DPA does not mention the processor's obligation to cooperate with the controller in cases of requests from supervisory authorities or data protection impact assessments.
5. e) Technical and organizational measures (Article 28(3)(e)): While the DPA mentions security measures, it should explicitly state that the technical and organizational measures shall be appropriate to the risk associated with the processing.
6. f) Data protection officer (Article 28(3)(f)): The DPA does not mention the processor's obligation to designate a data protection officer if required under Article 37 GDPR.
7. g) Restrictions on subcontracting (Article 28(3)(h)): The DPA allows the processor to engage sub-processors at its discretion, whereas Article 28(3)(h) requires that the data controller gives prior specific or general authorization for any subcontracting.
8. Clause 8: The DPA does not specify the information that should be provided to the controller about the sub-processor, such as the sub-processor's identity and the type of processing they will perform. This is in contrast with Article 28(3)(h), which requires the processor to provide written contracts or other legal acts binding the sub-processor to the data controller.

In summary, the DPA provided does not fully comply with the requirements set out in Article 28(3) GDPR regarding the written form, confidentiality, respect for data subjects' rights, cooperation with supervisory authorities, technical and organizational measures, data protection officer, restrictions on subcontracting, and information about sub-processors.
