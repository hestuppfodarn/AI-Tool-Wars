Here’s a breakdown of the DPA's compliance with Article 28(3) GDPR, highlighting missing elements and inaccuracies:

1.  **28(3)(a) – Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA):** Missing entirely. The DPA must include a DPIA if processing is likely to result in a high risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals. Given the nature of patient data and booking applications, a DPIA is *essential*.

2.  **28(3)(b) – Appointment of Data Protection Officer (DPO):** Not addressed. The DPA needs to specify if a DPO is appointed, their role, and contact details.

3.  **28(3)(c) – Information on data subjects’ rights:** Lacks detail. It should explicitly state how the Controller will inform patients about their rights (access, rectification, erasure, etc.) and provide mechanisms for exercising them.

4.  **28(3)(d) – Transfer of Data to Third Countries:** Clause 4 mentions transfers but doesn't specify safeguards (Standard Contractual Clauses or Binding Corporate Rules). This is a significant omission.

5. **28(3)(e) - Recipient of data:** The DPA does not identify who receives the data, beyond the processor. It needs to detail any third-party recipients and their purpose.


**Clause 8 Error:** Clause 8 incorrectly states that security measures are governed by Article 32 GDPR. Article 32 outlines *requirements* for security; Clause 8 should reference Annex 2 which details the specific measures implemented, not just a general reference to Article 32.

In summary, this DPA is severely deficient and fails to meet the core requirements of Article 28(3) GDPR due to its omission of a DPIA, lack of DPO information, insufficient detail on data subject rights, and failure to address international data transfers adequately.
